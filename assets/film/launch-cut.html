The direction, applied

Three techniques from the Apple News+ template carry the whole cut: clipping (the wall of classroom cards runs behind the numerals and is visible only inside them), card flips (shots 01 and 05 turn on their Y axis to reveal the data on the back), and text animation (every line rises through a mask, and the opening question types itself off the timeline). Shot 11 is a rapid-fire montage — seven hard cuts in three seconds, one idea each, ground colour changing every cut. Set in Inter with IBM Plex Mono, matching elmysolutions.org rather than the Figma file.

Scale as of September 2026

1,299 classrooms and 4,936 learner profiles, from the supplied Elmy platform audit. Other learner and performance figures in this illustrative film are sample data.

What I left out on purpose

Your global stats read 6.04 active-student rate and 35.54 average grade, but they're computed over 5 classrooms, not all 1,299. Unflattering and unrepresentative — so the film uses counts, which are true at any scale, not rates.

The score is a temp track

Every note is synthesised in the page and scheduled off the same clock as the picture, so the keystrokes, the send, the toggle snap and all seven cuts in the rush land exactly on frame. D major, 120bpm, with a real arrangement — break at 14:9, riser and half-bar drop-out before the rush, 16ths and open hats through it, then a pull-back. Keystrokes are synthesised mechanical keys — hard strike, hollow wooden body, metallic tink, release — on a jittered human cadence that also drives the on-screen text. Use it to lock the edit, then swap in a licensed track at the same tempo and hit points.

No real names

The dashboard is full of live student names. None of them appear here. Every card and row is generic SAT content, which is also what keeps the film usable in sales decks without a privacy review.
